1. Project Introduction & Overview

Last-mile delivery is the final leg of the supply chain journey where a shipment travels from a local transportation depot or fulfillment center to the customer's personal doorstep. In modern e-commerce and retail logistics, this stage represents 53% of overall shipping expenses.

The Smart Route Optimization System is an end-to-end web platform tailored for fleet dispatchers and drivers. It eliminates random manual sequencing by applying algorithmic nearest-neighbor ordering, dynamic traffic compensation, fuel consumption modeling, and live driver delivery lifecycle execution.

Core Engineering Objectives

  • Multi-Stop Sequencing: Automatically determine the most efficient delivery order from starting depot coordinates.
  • Priority Handling: Ensure urgent packages receive 40% distance preference over standard deliveries.
  • Traffic-Aware Routing: Adapt travel durations using simulated factors (Low 1.0x, Medium 1.2x, High 1.5x) with dynamic recalculation.
  • Fuel & Financial Transparency: Model fuel volume and expense across Petrol, Diesel, and Electric vehicles.
  • Driver Workflow: Provide field drivers with customer contacts, stop maps, and real-time status transitions (`Pending` → `Out for Delivery` → `Completed`).
  • Zero Paid API Dependency: Powered by Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap with zero Google Maps billing quotas.

3. Core Algorithms & Mathematical Formulas

A. Haversine Distance Formula

Because the Earth is an oblate spheroid, simple 2D Pythagorean distance ($d = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$) produces severe errors on GPS coordinates. The Haversine Formula calculates the great-circle spherical distance across the Earth's curved surface:

Mathematical Formulation:
$$\Delta \phi = \text{lat}_2 - \text{lat}_1 \quad (\text{radians})$$
$$\Delta \lambda = \text{lon}_2 - \text{lon}_1 \quad (\text{radians})$$
$$a = \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\text{lat}_1) \cdot \cos(\text{lat}_2) \cdot \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \lambda}{2}\right)$$
$$c = 2 \cdot \text{atan2}(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1-a})$$
$$d = R \cdot c \quad \text{where } R = 6,371\text{ km (Earth mean radius)}$$

B. Priority-Aware Nearest Neighbor Algorithm

The Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) is NP-hard. For last-mile delivery fleets of 5–30 stops, the Nearest Neighbor Greedy Heuristic executes in milliseconds ($O(N^2)$) and yields near-optimal routing.

To prevent urgent packages from waiting, our engine applies a Priority Distance Discount Factor (0.6x):

EffectiveDistance = RawHaversineDistance * (isUrgent ? 0.6 : 1.0)

An urgent package 10 km away is treated as only 6 km away during candidate evaluation, giving it priority over an 8 km standard package.

C. Traffic Simulation Model

Traffic delays are simulated using standardized multipliers:

  • LOW Traffic (1.0x): Free-flow urban transit, base speed = $35\text{ km/h}$.
  • MEDIUM Traffic (1.2x): Typical city daylight traffic, effective speed = $\frac{35}{1.2} \approx 29.2\text{ km/h}$.
  • HIGH Traffic (1.5x): Peak congestion, effective speed = $\frac{35}{1.5} \approx 23.3\text{ km/h}$.

D. Fuel Consumption & Cost Formulation

$$\text{Fuel Consumed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance (km)}}{\text{Vehicle Mileage (km/L)}}$$
$$\text{Total Fuel Cost (₹)} = \text{Fuel Consumed} \times \text{Unit Fuel Price (₹/L)}$$
$$\text{Cost Per Stop} = \frac{\text{Total Fuel Cost}}{\text{Stops Count}}$$

E. Stop-by-Stop ETA & Service Time Formulation

$$\text{Driving Minutes} = \left(\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Effective Speed}}\right) \times 60$$
$$\text{Service Minutes} = \text{Stops Count} \times 5\text{ mins (doorstep handover)}$$
$$\text{Total Trip Duration} = \text{Driving Minutes} + \text{Service Minutes}$$

F. Composite Route Scoring Formula

A lower route score indicates a more cost-effective and punctual route plan:

$$\text{Route Score} = (\text{Distance} \times 1.5) + \text{Traffic Penalty} + \text{Fuel Cost} - \text{Priority Bonus}$$
$$\text{Traffic Penalty} = (\text{Traffic Factor} - 1.0) \times 25.0$$
$$\text{Priority Bonus} = \text{Urgent Orders Count} \times 15.0$$

4. Complete REST API Reference

Authentication Endpoints

POST /api/auth/login — Authenticates user and returns JWT bearer token.

POST /api/auth/register — Creates new fleet user account.

GET /api/auth/me — Validates current JWT session token.

GET /api/auth/users — Retrieves all registered system users (Admin view).

POST /api/auth/seed-reset — Restores default sample logistics dataset.

Deliveries Endpoints

GET /api/deliveries — Lists all delivery orders.

POST /api/deliveries — Creates a new delivery order with GPS coordinates.

PUT /api/deliveries/:id/status — Updates delivery status (`out_for_delivery`, `completed`, `failed`).

POST /api/deliveries/:id/issue — Logs a field issue/delay (traffic, breakdown, unavailable).

DELETE /api/deliveries/:id — Removes order from database.

Route Optimization Endpoints

GET /api/routes/data — Returns drivers, vehicles, and pending deliveries for the planner form.

POST /api/routes/preview — Runs Nearest Neighbor preview without database persistence.

POST /api/routes/optimize — Runs optimization, saves Route document, and links driver/vehicle.

POST /api/routes/:id/recalculate — Recomputes route metrics dynamically when traffic factor changes.

GET /api/routes/driver/:driverId — Returns the active assigned route for a specific driver.

Analytics Endpoints

GET /api/analytics — Returns On-Time Rate, Completion Rate, Vehicle Utilization, and Planned vs. Actual Variance.

GET /api/dashboard/summary — Returns aggregated active fleet counts and total route mileage.

6. Viva Voce & Technical Interview Guide

Q1: Why did you choose the Haversine formula instead of Euclidean distance?

Answer: Euclidean distance assumes a flat 2D Cartesian plane. Because the Earth is a sphere, lines of longitude converge toward the poles ($1^\circ$ of longitude is smaller near the poles than at the equator). The Haversine formula calculates the true great-circle distance along the spherical surface using spherical trigonometry.

Q2: How does the algorithm ensure Urgent orders are not delayed?

Answer: During each step of the Nearest Neighbor candidate evaluation, urgent deliveries receive an effective distance discount factor of $0.6\times$. This means an urgent stop 10 km away is evaluated as if it were 6 km away, prioritizing it over a 7 km standard delivery unless the standard delivery is directly on the way.

Q3: Why use Leaflet.js instead of the Google Maps JavaScript API?

Answer: Google Maps requires a paid Google Cloud account, API keys, and incurs billing limits. Leaflet.js is lightweight ($~42\text{ KB}$), 100% open-source, uses free OpenStreetMap tiles, and requires zero paid credentials, making it optimal for resilient, reproducible academic MVPs.

Q4: How does dynamic route recalculation work when traffic increases?

Answer: When the traffic factor increases from Medium (1.2x) to High (1.5x), the effective transit speed drops from $\approx 29\text{ km/h}$ to $\approx 23\text{ km/h}$. The endpoint recalculates driving minutes, applies doorstep service times, updates the clock ETAs, increases the congestion penalty in the route score, and re-renders the map polyline in red.
